      Before you report: preserve the evidence

      1. Don't confront or contact the person. It can put you and the child at risk and can tip them off to delete evidence.
      2. Don't delete anything. Keep the messages, accounts and devices exactly as they are.
      3. Screenshot usernames, profile URLs, messages, and the date and time.
      4. Don't forward or download sexual images of a minor, even to report them. Describe where you saw them; investigators will collect them lawfully.
      5. Write down what you know: who, what, where, when, and how you found out.

      Incident notes (stays on this device)

        Grooming warning signs

        Most children are harmed by someone they know, and much grooming now starts online. A registry search alone doesn't keep a child safe. These behaviors are what to watch for.

        An adult who…

        • Seeks out time alone with a specific child
        • Gives gifts, money or privileges with no clear reason
        • Pushes physical boundaries (tickling, wrestling, lap-sitting) after a child says no
        • Tells a child to keep secrets from parents
        • Undermines parents ("they don't understand you like I do")

        Online, watch for…

        • A new "friend" who moves the chat to private or disappearing-message apps
        • Requests for photos, or offers of game currency or gift cards
        • Flattery, then guilt, then threats (sextortion)
        • Requests to meet in person

        In your child…

        • Secrecy about devices or a new contact
        • New gifts, money or accounts they can't explain
        • Withdrawal, anxiety, or sexual knowledge beyond their age
        • Sudden fear of a particular person or place

        What to tell kids

        • "You will never be in trouble for telling me."
        • No adult should ask you to keep a secret from me.
        • Someone threatening to share your pictures is committing a crime. It isn't your fault.
        • Teens can use Take It Down to get images removed.

        Data source

        Load registry data

        CSV with header: id,name,address,lat,lng,offense,tier,registry_url,last_verified. Use an export your state registry provides, or a licensed data provider. Check your state's terms of use first. Many states restrict how registry data may be republished.

        Rules of use

        • Registry information is provided for public safety awareness only.
        • Using it to threaten, harass, intimidate or harm anyone is a crime in every state, and it makes communities less safe.
        • Records can be outdated or wrong. Always confirm on the official registry before acting.
        • If you see a violation, report it to law enforcement. Never confront the person yourself.

        Before you continue

        SafeWatch shows public registry information for child-safety awareness.

        By continuing, you agree not to use it to harass, threaten, or harm anyone. That is illegal and puts families at risk. Report concerns to law enforcement.
